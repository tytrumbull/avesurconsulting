Market Opportunity Analysis

Many small and mid-sized companies could grow through export or import but have never had the time to look properly. Small teams can build big things now, if they enter the right market the right way. We do the research and give you the facts to decide: go, wait or don't.

Discuss this service
You come to us with
A product or service, and a question: is Canada, Mexico or the United States worth entering, and what would it take?
We do
Analyze demand and trade flows for your HS product codes, map tariff and non-tariff barriers (standards, certifications, labelling, registrations), benchmark competitors and pricing, identify likely buyers, distributors and procurement channels, and estimate the cost and timeline to enter.
You receive
A market opportunity report: demand and trade-flow summary, barrier and cost-to-enter matrix, competitor and pricing benchmark, a named list of target buyers and partners, and a go / wait / no-go recommendation with a 90-day entry plan.
Finished when
The report is delivered and walked through with you. If you proceed, it becomes the scope for Regulatory & Compliance Assessment or Cross-Border Market Entry.
Not included:
Guaranteed sales, legal opinions or customs classification rulings. Market data comes from public trade statistics and sources cited in the report.

Audit, Assessment & Assurance

We take a defined set of requirements and independently determine whether an organization, system, process or product actually meets them. We did not build it and we are not selling the fix, so the answer is a true third-party view.

Discuss this service
You come to us with
A requirement set (NIST SP 800-53 or 800-171, the Cyber Centre's ITSP.10 series, ISO/IEC 27001, contract security clauses or internal policy) and the question: do we actually meet it?
We do
Map controls to evidence, interview, inspect and test where scoped, and rate each requirement.
You receive
An assessment report: findings by requirement, evidence references, risk ratings and prioritized remediation recommendations.
Finished when
The report is delivered and walked through with your team. A fixed-scope retest is optional.
Not included:
Fixing what we assessed, issuing certifications, legal opinions, or authorization decisions, which belong to your authorizing official or accredited certification body.

Specialized Applications
North America

Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement (CUSMA / USMCA / T-MEC) Trade Compliance

Rules of Origin & Origin Procedures (Chapters 4 and 5)

  • Origin analysis for goods: costed bill of materials (BOM), tariff shift and regional value content (RVC)
  • Supplier origin reviews and supplier declarations
  • Preparing and reviewing certifications of origin (no prescribed form; minimum data elements in Annex 5-A) and the records needed for a CBSA or CBP origin verification

Digital Trade & Data (Chapter 19)

  • Data residency and cross-border data transfer assessments against your contracts, privacy law and sector rules
  • What Chapter 19 does and doesn't do: it limits what governments can require of you, and it doesn't cover government procurement

Mexico: Product & Import Requirements

For Canadian and US companies selling physical products into Mexico.

  • Identifying mandatory NOMs for your tariff codes, including labelling NOMs such as NOM-050-SCFI and NOM-051 for food and beverages, plus voluntary NMX standards buyers ask for
  • Certification and testing paths with accredited certification bodies and labs, including COFEPRIS sanitary registrations for health-related products
  • Importer readiness: Padrón de Importadores status, IMMEX program obligations and the documents your customs broker (agente aduanal) will need

Mexico: NOM Conformity & COFEPRIS Readiness

Many NOMs are pass or fail: a product, label or installation meets defined values or it doesn't. We check your file the way the certification body, inspection unit or COFEPRIS will, before they see it.

  • Pre-assessment of technical files for NOMs that require certification, such as NOM-001-SCFI for electronic equipment safety and NOM-019 for IT equipment, so lab testing happens once
  • Label checks against NOM-024-SCFI (electronics), NOM-050-SCFI, NOM-051 (food and beverages) and NOM-141 (cosmetics) before goods ship, to avoid retention at customs
  • Medical device registration holders: technovigilance unit set-up, procedures and the five-year technovigilance report COFEPRIS requires for registration renewal (NOM-240-SSA1)
  • Telecom and wireless equipment: tracking certification requirements as approvals move from the IFT to Mexico's new telecommunications regulator, the CRT
  • Our role: we prepare and verify. Certificates, inspections and test reports come from accredited bodies approved under Mexico's Ley de Infraestructura de la Calidad.

Canada & US: Product & Import Requirements

For Mexican companies selling physical products into Canada or the US.

  • Canadian bilingual (English/French) labelling, plus Quebec's French-language requirements
  • Product safety certification, such as electrical products certified by an SCC-accredited body, and US agency requirements (for example FDA or FCC) where they apply
  • Importer-of-record setup: CBSA CARM registration and release-prior-to-payment security for non-resident importers

Requirements & Procurement Readiness

Hand us the RFP, regulation, standard or contract. We turn it into a clear map of every obligation, the evidence each one needs, where you stand, and what to do next. Valuable on its own, even before a full audit.

Discuss this service
You come to us with
An RFP, Security Requirements Check List (SRCL), contract clause, standard, regulation or supply-arrangement application with money or eligibility at stake.
We do
Extract every obligation, map each to the evidence it requires, check your current status, and flag gaps and owners.
You receive
An obligations matrix, a gap list and a next-action plan, with an optional bid-compliance review.
Finished when
The matrix and plan are delivered, with an optional re-check before submission.
Not included:
Writing your entire bid or signing attestations on your behalf.

Procurement vehicles: ProServices Supply Arrangement (E60ZT-180024/C) submission under review; SBIPS and the Federal AI Source List (ITQ WS4286933967) on our procurement roadmap. Eligible federal services engagements up to $40,000 may be contracted non-competitively under section 6(b) of the Government Contracts Regulations, subject to the contracting authority's rules and approvals.

Government Procurement Access

Selling to governments across borders depends on which agreement covers the purchase.

  • Mexico: Registering on Compras MX, the federal procurement platform that replaced CompraNet, and in the Registro Único de Participantes
  • Canadian suppliers: Access to Mexican federal procurement comes through CPTPP. CUSMA's procurement chapter covers only Mexico and the US.
  • Before you bid: The buying entity, the goods or services and the value thresholds decide whether trade agreement access applies
  • CFE: Mexico's Federal Electricity Commission runs its own procurement under general provisions in force since August 20, 2026, with technical requirements set in each tender

What Avesur Is Not

  • Not a managed service provider. Scoped engagements that finish, not indefinite IT management.
  • Not a large implementation consultancy. A small, senior team on a defined problem.
  • Not a law firm. We interpret requirements operationally; legal opinions go to lawyers.
