How We Work

Every engagement follows the same documented process, whether we are assessing a cloud environment against ITSP.10.033, preparing a product file for a Mexican certification body or sizing a market. You know what we will look at, how we will test it, what you will receive and when we are finished.

The engagement lifecycle

Six phases, each with a defined output you can review before the next one begins.

  1. 01

    Scope and Rules of Engagement

    We confirm the objective, the market or regulator, the systems, products or sites in scope, and what is out. Access, contacts, timelines and confidentiality are agreed in writing, and any prior involvement that could affect our independence is declared.

    Output:Signed scope and rules of engagement

  2. 02

    Requirements Baseline

    We identify every requirement that applies: laws, technical regulations such as Mexico's NOMs, security control catalogues, trade agreement rules, contract clauses and buyer expectations. Each one is broken down into a measurable criterion and the evidence that would prove it.

    Output:Requirements register and evidence request list

  3. 03

    Evidence Collection and Testing

    We use the three assessment methods defined in NIST SP 800-53A: examine (documents, configurations, records), interview (the people who run the process) and test (the system or product itself). Technical security testing follows NIST SP 800-115. Where we sample, the sample and how it was chosen are documented.

    Output:Assessment workbook with evidence references

  4. 04

    Analysis and Risk Rating

    Each requirement is rated met, partially met or not met. Every gap is rated by likelihood and impact, using NIST SP 800-30 or the Harmonized TRA Methodology (TRA-1) for security, and ISO 31000 principles for regulatory and market risk.

    Output:Findings register with risk ratings

  5. 05

    Reporting

    A senior assessor reviews every finding before release. Each one traces to the requirement, the evidence and the reasoning behind its rating. Reports are written for two readers: an executive summary for decision-makers and technical detail for the people doing the work, in English, French or Spanish.

    Output:Assessment report, executive summary and remediation roadmap

  6. 06

    Remediation Support and Closure

    We answer questions while your team closes the gaps, then verify the fixes against the same criteria. When an accredited body, regulator or buyer takes over, your evidence is already organized the way they expect to see it.

    Output:Closure verification and evidence package

How we turn a standard into a test

Technical standards are measurable: a requirement says a value must be met, a document must exist or a control must work. We break every standard we assess into the same structure. That structure is what lets us automate the repetitive parts and keep the judgment calls with a senior assessor.

  1. 1Standard
  2. 2Section
  3. 3Requirement
  4. 4Measurable criterion
  5. 5Evidence
  6. 6Result

Example: Spanish-language product information

Standard
NOM-024-SCFI-2013, commercial information for electronic and electrical products
Requirement
Commercial information appears in Spanish on the product, packaging or instructions
Measurable criterion
Product name, importer name and address, country of origin and electrical ratings are present and in Spanish
Evidence
Label artwork and photos of production samples
Result
Pass, or a list of corrections before the goods ship

Stored in a machine-readable format such as NIST's OSCAL, the same structure drives evidence requests, checklists, dashboards and reassessments. Automation drafts. A senior assessor decides.

Frameworks we work from

We use the published methods your buyers, regulators and certification bodies already recognize, so our findings carry over into their process.

Security assessment and testing

  • NIST SP 800-53A: assessment procedures (examine, interview, test)
  • NIST SP 800-115: technical security testing
  • ITSG-33 and ITSP.10.033: Government of Canada security controls
  • ITSP.10.171: CPCSC controls for defence suppliers
  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

Risk

  • Harmonized TRA Methodology (TRA-1): threat and risk assessment
  • NIST SP 800-30: risk assessment
  • ISO 31000: risk management principles

Audit practice

  • ISO 19011: guidelines for auditing management systems
  • ISO/IEC 27001 and 27007: information security management and its audit

Conformity assessment

  • ISO/IEC 17065, 17020 and 17025: the standards product certifiers, inspection bodies and test labs are accredited to, by SCC in Canada and EMA in Mexico
  • Mexico's Ley de Infraestructura de la Calidad: how NOM conformity is assessed and by whom
  • We prepare you for these bodies. We are not one of them.

Trade and supply chain

  • CUSMA Chapters 4 and 5: rules of origin and origin procedures
  • WCO SAFE Framework: the basis for trusted-trader programs, including CBSA Partners in Protection, US CTPAT and Mexico's Authorized Economic Operator (OEA)

Privacy

  • PIPEDA and Quebec Law 25: including privacy impact assessments
  • Mexico's LFPDPPP (2025): personal data held by private parties
  • NIST Privacy Framework

What every engagement includes

  • Independence

    We never assess work we built or remediated for the same client. Any prior involvement is declared at scoping.

  • Traceability

    Every finding points to a requirement, the evidence examined and the reason for its rating. Nothing in a report rests on opinion alone.

  • Expert review

    Automation speeds up collection and mapping. A senior assessor reviews and signs off every finding before it reaches you.

  • Confidentiality

    Client evidence is handled under written confidentiality terms. Avesur holds an active Secret (Level II) Facility Security Clearance.

  • Clear boundaries

    We are not a law firm, customs broker, laboratory or certification body. Where you need one, we coordinate them and hand over evidence they can use.

  • Three languages

    Interviews, evidence review and reports in English, French or Spanish, so nothing is lost between your team, your buyer and the regulator.
